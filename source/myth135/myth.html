Myth or evidence?

Five claims about sound, checked by a doctor.

Online, tones are said to heal, cleanse and repair. Here is what studies actually show, and what remains.

01528 Hz repairs your DNA.No evidence

The claim A “Solfeggio” tone at 528 Hz is said to heal, repair DNA and lower stress.

What studies show The best-known study had nine healthy people. After music tuned to 528 Hz their saliva cortisol fell; after 440 Hz it did not. Its own authors write that the effects ascribed to Solfeggio tones have no scientific basis. No study shows DNA repair from listening.

What remains Calm music can lower stress. Whether the tuning matters cannot be said from nine people.

Sources: Akimoto K et al. · Health 2018;10:1159–1170

021150 Hz kills parasites.No evidence

The claim Five minutes a day of a 1150 Hz tone through headphones is said to destroy parasites, after the inventor Royal Rife.

What studies show A fact check found no evidence for it. To harm a parasite, the sound would have to be loud enough to damage your hearing first. Cancer research charities see no reliable evidence for Rife machines either.

What remains Sound can destroy tissue, but only as focused ultrasound in a clinic: histotripsy. That is not listening.

Sources: AAP FactCheck · 12 Nov 2024 · Full Fact · Rife machines

03A CIA file proves that humming heals.No evidence

The claim A declassified file is said to show that humming and special tapes bring the body back to perfect health.

What studies show The file is a 1983 assessment by a US Army officer of the Monroe Institute’s “Gateway” course, now public in the CIA reading room. It is an opinion paper, not a trial, and it measures nothing.

What remains Humming does do something measurable: it raises nitric oxide in the nose about fifteen-fold. Try the minute above.

Sources: McDonnell WM · Analysis and Assessment of Gateway Process, 1983

04Binaural beats tune your brainwaves.Mixed evidence

The claim Two slightly different tones, one in each ear, are said to pull the brain into calm, focus or sleep.

What studies show A 2019 meta-analysis of 22 studies found a medium effect on memory, attention, anxiety and pain. A 2023 review of 14 EEG studies found that the brain does not reliably follow the beat. Some careful trials found no effect at all.

What remains Perhaps a small help with anxiety, by a route nobody can yet explain. That is why it needs careful trials, like the two Dr. Martin is designing for radiotherapy.

Sources: Garcia-Argibay M et al. · Psychol Res 2019;83:357–372 · Ingendoh RM et al. · PLOS ONE 2023;18(5):e0286023

05Music lowers stress.Solid evidence

The claim Listening to music calms the body and the mind.

What studies show A 2020 meta-analysis of 104 randomised trials with 9,617 people found a clear effect, on the body and on how stressed people feel. A Cochrane review found that music before surgery may lower anxiety, although many trials had weaknesses.

What remains This one holds. No magic frequency is needed: music you like is enough.

Sources: de Witte M et al. · Health Psychol Rev 2020;14(2):294–324 · Bradt J et al. · Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2013;CD006908
