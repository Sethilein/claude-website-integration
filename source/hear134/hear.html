Listening lab

Your ears, your breath.

Two small experiments from the clinic: find where a rising tone fades for you, and hum for one minute. Each shows what research knows, and where it stops.

Experiment 1 · Hearing sweep

Where does the tone fade for you?

A quiet tone climbs from low to very high. Tap the moment you no longer hear it.

—

Turn your volume down first. Headphones work best.

The top of the hearing range comes down with age, first at the highest tones. Speakers, headphones and the browser cut the top as well, so this is a feeling for your range, not a hearing test. If voices sound muffled or your ears ring, see an ear doctor.

Experiment 2 · One minute of humming

Hum for one minute.

Breathe in through your nose, then hum softly as you breathe out, lips closed. Six rounds of ten seconds, with a low tone to hum along or in silence.

Six rounds · one minute

While you hum, nitric oxide in the nose rises about fifteen-fold. It comes from the sinuses: the hum moves their air.

A 2024 study found that this does not sharpen short-term thinking or the processing of emotions. Take it as a minute to slow down, not as a treatment.
