03 · Sound made visible

A note you can look at.

Sand on a steel plate gathers where the plate stands still. Change the note and the pattern changes with it. This is the thing he puts on stage behind the violin, and the thing he studies by day.

Drag the slider. The grains move to the lines where the plate is not moving — the higher the note, the finer the figure.

    09 · The other side
    Precision & harmony

    The same hands, twelve hours earlier.

    By day he is a specialist in radiation oncology at the university hospital in Halle: millimetres, protocols, patients who have to lie still. That discipline is the reason a set can be built on a loop that must land exactly on the beat — and the improvisation of a night is the reason he can keep his judgement when a plan on the ward has to change.

    Dr. Martin Zschornak · Radiation oncology · Halle (Saale), Germany

    What sound doesto people.

    A specialist physician in radiation oncology who has played to crowds on six continents. He treats patients, teaches, and leads a trial on whether sound can lower the fear of lying still in a radiotherapy machine — clear about what is known, and where it ends.

    01 · Clinical focus

    Precision medicine, and the person inside the machine.

    Radiotherapy is measured in millimetres and minutes. The part that is harder to measure is what the minutes feel like for the person lying still inside the device. He works on both.

    02 · Publications and press

    His published research is laboratory work on brain tumour cells. It is not about music. The press pieces are about a doctor who also plays.
